The 58th Church of Hispania was an all-welcoming church. Its highest-ranking human authority was the Pope himself, the Church of Hispania being Catholic in its full, unambiguous sense. The building was clean and warm. It smelled of candle wax and old wood and something floral Han-Ri couldn't identify.

They found a pew and sat down. Around them, the congregation settled in — a mix of faces, a range of ages, the general texture of a community in which people had, for various reasons, ended up here on a Tuesday-adjacent March morning.

The mass began.

They sang together:

*"Jesus Kries! Jesus Kries! Jee Sus Jee Sus Kries Kries!"*

Han-Ri did not have a strong singing voice. Maiden-Dick did. She always had. He'd forgotten that, somehow. He heard her voice beside him and felt, for just a moment, the specific tenderness of remembering something you'd let go without meaning to.

It was during this silence that Maiden-Dick's eyes drifted across the pew, and a man looked back.

He was unremarkable in the way of a person who has no need to be remarkable — around five feet nine inches tall, warm-faced, with the kind of easy, open presence that the cold and distant affect of Northern Germanic manner had never quite cultivated. He smiled. She smiled back. Han-Ri, focused on the altar, did not notice.

Later, when the mass ended and the congregation dispersed into the courtyard light, Han-Ri excused himself to use the restroom. This was a simple thing. A biological fact. But in the geography of a marriage coming apart at its seams, even small absences become consequential.

When he returned, the man and Maiden-Dick were deep in conversation. They were laughing about something. It was the easy laughter of people who have discovered they share a frequency.

Han-Ri stopped at the entrance to the courtyard. He watched for a moment.

An anger moved through him — not a loud anger, but the quiet, slow kind, the kind that settles into the jaw and behind the eyes. His face flushed. He gritted his teeth. He did not interrupt. It was a public setting, a church courtyard, a day meant for something else. So he stood by the doorway and waited, holding himself carefully, the way a person holds something they are afraid of dropping.

When Maiden-Dick finally noticed him and made her way back, she said nothing about the conversation. Han-Ri said nothing about what he'd seen.

They walked to the car together.
